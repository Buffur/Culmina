   6  # Research
   7  
   8  Run a fresh UX research scan for the product the user specifies.
   9  
  10  Focus on current, evidence-backed user problems. Prioritize logged-in product experience, self-serve flows, onboarding, docs/help, developer experience, support friction, and product workflows.
  11  
  12  ## Critical Overrides
  13  
  14  - Refer to the Plugin router [$index](../index/SKILL.md) before proceeding.
  15  - Follow [$critical-overrides](../../references/critical-overrides.md).
  16  
  17  ## User Context
  18  
  19  Before starting, load [$user-context](../user-context/SKILL.md) and run its preflight script when local shell access is available.
  20  
  21  Use saved product URLs, Figma files, screenshots, reference images, codebase paths, Storybook, tokens, design systems, brand assets, component refs, browser preferences, and share targets as grounding material when relevant.
  22  
  23  Do not inspect every saved reference. Inspect only what the current task needs.
  24  
  25  ## Contract
  26  
  27  - Restate the product, audience, time horizon, and research scope before scanning.
  28  - Use public sources by default. Use internal sources when the connectors are available and the user request allows it.
  29  - Cite sources wherever available.
  30  - Separate observed evidence from inference.
  31  - Do not overclaim from anecdotes.
  32  - Do not return a dump of complaints. Tell a clear product story.
  33  - Say clearly when source access is missing or weak.
  34  
  35  ## Workflow
  36  
  37  1. Restate the research scope.
  38  
  39  2. Search public sources:
  40  
  41  - Reddit
  42  - X/Twitter
  43  - Hacker News
  44  - Stack Overflow
  45  - GitHub issues/discussions
  46  - forums, blogs, reviews, YouTube comments, and developer communities where relevant
  47  
  48  3. Search internal sources when available:
  49  
  50  - Slack
  51  - Gong
  52  - Notion
  53  - Google Drive/docs
  54  - Linear/Jira/GitHub
  55  - support or CRM notes if available
  56  
  57  4. Cluster evidence into the highest-signal UX problems.
  58  
  59  5. Separate:
  60  
  61  - product UI/workflow friction
  62  - docs/help friction
  63  - onboarding friction
  64  - account, billing, permissions, or setup friction
  65  - developer/API/SDK friction
  66  - reliability/performance issues
  67  - feature requests
  68  
  69  6. Rank problems by severity, frequency, confidence, and product leverage.
  70  
  71  7. Tell a clear product story.
  72  
  73  ## Output
  74  
  75  Default to an in-chat research brief unless the user asks for another format.
  76  
  77  Include:
  78  
  79  - Executive read: the core story in 5-7 sentences.
  80  - Ranked UX problems: for each problem, include the problem, user goal, surface, what breaks, evidence, severity, frequency signal, confidence, and recommended product move.
  81  - Source map: what was searched, what each source contributed, and where signal was weak.
  82  - Opportunity map: group recommendations into fix this week, fix this quarter, and needs deeper research.
  83  
  84  ## Rules
  85  
  86  - Use citations wherever available.
  87  - Do not overclaim from anecdotes.
  88  - Separate loud complaints from frequent problems.
  89  - Separate UX friction from missing features.
  90  - Separate reliability/performance issues from UX workflow issues.
  91  - Mark internal-only evidence separately from public evidence.
  92  - Keep the brief sharp, specific, and easy to consume.
